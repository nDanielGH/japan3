## What Is Hekara Yokocho?

![hekara yokocho in Kawaguchiko](https://slowtripjapan.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/IMG_7174-1024x768.jpeg)Photo by Slow Trip Japan

### A New Nightlife Spot in Kawaguchiko

Hekara Yokocho is a newly opened food and drink spot in the Kawaguchiko area designed for people who want to enjoy the evening after a day of sightseeing. Instead of being just one restaurant, it offers a yokocho-style atmosphere where visitors can eat, drink, and move between different places in a casual setting.

In Japan, the word _yokocho_ often refers to small alley-style dining areas filled with character, local food, and a social atmosphere. Hekara Yokocho brings that kind of energy to Kawaguchiko, which is something the area has not had many options for in the past.

For travelers staying overnight, it creates a much better nighttime option than simply returning to the hotel after dinner.

### Why It’s Becoming Popular with Visitors

When I visited, one of the first things I noticed was how many international tourists were already there. That usually means people are sharing the location online, recommending it to friends, or finding it naturally while exploring the area.

The atmosphere feels welcoming and easy to enter, even for first-time visitors. Instead of a formal restaurant experience, it feels more casual and relaxed, which is ideal for travelers who just want to enjoy food, drinks, and the local mood without much planning.

Because Kawaguchiko continues to attract more overseas visitors every year, places like Hekara Yokocho are likely to become even more popular in the future. It offers something many travelers are looking for — a fun and convenient place to spend the evening near Mt. Fuji.

## Where It Is and How to Get There

### Location Near Kawaguchiko Area

Hekara Yokocho is located in the Kawaguchiko area at:

**[1807-1 Kodachi, Fujikawaguchiko-machi, Minamitsuru-gun, Yamanashi 401-0302, Japan](https://maps.app.goo.gl/KKbbqFonHLkxBKsj6)**

The location is slightly away from the busiest tourist center, which helps give it a more local and relaxed atmosphere compared with the station area. It is still close enough to reach easily from most hotels around Lake Kawaguchi, making it a convenient evening destination after sightseeing.

Because it is not hidden deep in the countryside, it is a realistic option even for short stays in the Mt. Fuji area.

### Can You Walk from Hotels or the Station?

From Kawaguchiko Station, walking takes around **30 minutes**, depending on your route and pace. For some travelers, that can be manageable during good weather, but it may feel long after a full day of sightseeing or if you plan to stay late.

If you are staying at a hotel closer to the lake or nearby roads, the walk may be shorter. However, many visitors will likely find it easier to use a taxi, rental car, or bicycle rather than walking from the station at night.

For travelers who enjoy evening walks, it can still be an option — just be prepared for a longer return trip.

For travelers who want more flexibility during their stay, renting a car can make getting around Kawaguchiko much easier.

## My Visit to Hekara Yokocho

### First Impressions and Atmosphere

One of the most interesting things about Hekara Yokocho is its location. It sits quietly in a residential neighborhood, surrounded by houses, so it almost feels like a hidden local spot rather than a major tourist attraction.

As soon as I arrived, I could hear conversations, laughter, and the energy of people enjoying their evening. That immediately created a welcoming and lively atmosphere. Instead of feeling overly polished or commercial, it felt natural and relaxed — the kind of place where people come to enjoy food, drinks, and good company.

This contrast between the quiet neighborhood outside and the cheerful atmosphere inside makes the place feel even more memorable.

### How Crowded It Was

I visited on a weekday, so it was busy but not so crowded that you could not get in. There was still enough space to move around comfortably and enjoy the atmosphere without feeling overwhelmed.

Many groups were already inside eating, drinking, and having lively conversations. The place clearly had energy, but it still felt manageable and enjoyable rather than stressful.

On weekends, holidays, or during peak travel seasons, it would likely feel much busier. Visiting on a weekday may be the best option if you prefer a more relaxed experience.

### What Kind of Visitors Were There

What stood out most during my visit was that there were surprisingly few Japanese visitors. Most of the people there seemed to be international travelers enjoying a night out in Kawaguchiko.

That makes sense, as there are many guesthouses, small hotels, and accommodations nearby. It felt like many visitors were staying in the area and coming here for drinks or dinner after a day of sightseeing around Mt. Fuji.

I also noticed people arriving in different ways — some came by car, some walked, and others arrived by rental bicycle. That variety shows how popular the area is with travelers using different styles of transportation.

If you’re staying nearby, _Hekara Yokocho_ is an easy and fun evening option after check-in.

## Things to Know Before You Go

### Best Time to Visit

_Kawaguchiko_ is especially popular during the summer months, when many people visit to escape the heat found in larger Japanese cities. Because the area is cooler and surrounded by nature, it becomes a busy seasonal destination for both domestic and international travelers.

Other peak periods such as **Golden Week (late April to early May), Obon holidays in August, and the New Year season** can also bring large crowds to the Mt. Fuji area. During these times, restaurants and nightlife spots may become much busier than usual.

If you prefer a more relaxed experience with fewer people, visiting _Hekara Yokocho_ on a weekday is usually the best option.

### Busy Hours and Weekends

Weekends are naturally busier, especially because many people in Japan go out for food and drinks on Friday and Saturday nights.

If you visit during those evenings, expect a livelier atmosphere, more customers, and potentially longer wait times depending on the venue. This can be fun if you enjoy energetic nightlife, but it may feel crowded if you prefer something quieter.

For a calmer experience, weekday evenings are usually easier and more comfortable.

### Cash, Card, and Local Tips

Most places appeared to accept credit cards, which is convenient for travelers who do not want to carry too much cash.

However, during my visit, one _karaoke bar An_ inside the area was **cash only**, so it is still a good idea to bring some Japanese yen just in case. Smaller shops or bars in Japan sometimes still prefer cash payments.

As a general local tip, carrying a little cash while also having a card available is the safest and easiest option for a night out in Kawaguchiko.
